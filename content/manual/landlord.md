The **Landlord** section helps you collect unpaid rent. It works differently for homes and for business premises, so you choose which kind of tenancy you have first:

- **Residential tenancy:** homes, suites and apartments under the *Residential Tenancy Act*. Rent disputes go to the Residential Tenancy Branch (RTB), not the Civil Resolution Tribunal or Small Claims.
- **Commercial lease:** shops, offices and industrial space. The RTB doesn't cover these. You can sue for the arrears or use the remedies in your lease.

> **Important:** This is legal information about BC tenancy procedure, not legal advice. Never change the locks on a residential tenant, remove their belongings or cut off services. If you're unsure, click **Request lawyer review** on the file.

# Start a file

Click **Landlord** in the menu, then **Start a residential file** or **Start a commercial file**. Fill in:

- The **landlord's legal name**, any agent or property manager, and the landlord's **address for service**, phone and email.
- Every **tenant's full name**, one per line, exactly as on the tenancy agreement or lease. For a commercial lease, also list any **indemnifiers or guarantors**.
- The **rental unit or premises** address, including the unit number and postal code.
- The **monthly rent**, the **day it's due**, the date the **first unpaid rent** was due, how many **months are unpaid**, and anything already **paid toward those months**.
- Residential: any **unpaid utilities** owed to you and the date you gave a written demand for them. Commercial: how many **days rent can be unpaid before the lease allows re-entry** (often 15).

Click **Open the file**. Your files are listed on the Landlord page with the next date coming up.

# The rent ledger

The **Rent ledger** lists each charge, what's been paid and what's still owing. Use **Add a charge** for each new month of rent, utilities or another charge, and **Record a payment** when money comes in. Payments are applied to the oldest charge first, the same way the RTB's worksheet reads. The total at the bottom is what goes on your letter and forms.

> **Tip:** If a residential tenant pays only part of what they owe after you've served a 10 Day Notice, give a receipt that says the money is accepted "for use and occupancy only" and that the notice still stands. Add that wording in the payment's note so you have a record.

# The rent demand letter

The software generates a rent demand letter from your ledger, on your letterhead. Click **Preview letter** to check it, then **Send rent demand**. If you've connected your mailbox and added the tenant's email, it's emailed from your own account. Otherwise print it and deliver it or send it by registered mail.

- A residential letter gives the tenant 5 days to pay and explains that you may serve a 10 Day Notice and apply to the RTB. It is not a notice to end the tenancy.
- A commercial letter gives the tenant 10 days to pay, reserves your rights under the lease, and is copied to any indemnifiers.

Each rent demand counts as one letter toward your plan's monthly limit ({{STANDARD_LIMIT}} letters a month on Standard).

# Residential: the RTB steps

The checklist on a residential file follows the RTB's own steps for unpaid rent:

1. Record the rent owing and every payment.
2. Send a rent demand letter (optional, but it often gets rent paid).
3. If the tenant owes you utilities, give a written demand and wait 30 days.
4. Prepare the **10 Day Notice to End Tenancy for Unpaid Rent or Utilities (RTB-30)** and upload your signed copy to be checked.
5. Serve it and complete the **Proof of Service (RTB-34)**.
6. Wait 5 days. If the tenant pays everything owing in that time, the notice is cancelled. If they dispute it, the RTB holds a hearing.
7. If they didn't pay or dispute, apply online for a **Direct Request (RTB-12L-DR)** with the **Direct Request Worksheet (RTB-46)**. The filing fee is {{RTB_FILING_FEE}}, and you can ask for it back from the tenant.
8. Serve the Direct Request package on each tenant within 3 days of it becoming available, and complete the **Landlord's Proof of Service (RTB-44)**.
9. Get the decision: an order of possession and a monetary order.
10. Serve the orders on the tenant.
11. If the tenant doesn't leave, file the order of possession in the BC Supreme Court for a writ of possession. Only a court bailiff can remove a tenant.
12. Collect the monetary order through Small Claims with the **Confirmation of Service (RTB-21)**.
13. When the tenancy ends, return the deposits or apply to keep them within 15 days after the later of the end of the tenancy and the tenant giving you their forwarding address in writing.

Steps that need proof take an upload, which is checked against the file. See [Uploading documents and automatic checks](/help/enforcement).

# Residential: details for the RTB forms

Under **Details for the RTB forms**, fill in each section and click **Save**. Each section shows **complete** or **needs details**:

- **Landlord:** legal name, agent, address for service, phone and email.
- **Tenants:** every tenant's name, phone and email, and whether they agreed in writing to be served by email.
- **Rental unit:** unit, street, city and postal code.
- **Tenancy terms:** start date, whether there's a written agreement, whether the tenant still lives there, rent and due day, the last Notice of Rent Increase, and the security and pet damage deposits with the dates they were paid.
- **Utilities:** the date of your written demand.
- **10 Day Notice (RTB-30) and how it was served (RTB-34):** the date you signed it, the effective date, whether the tenant disputed it, and how it was served: date, time, method, who served it, who it was handed to, the address, any registered mail tracking number and a witness.
- **Direct Request (RTB-12L-DR) and serving the package (RTB-44):** the date you applied, the RTB file number, the date the package became available, and how you served it.
- **Decision and orders:** the decision date, the order of possession date, the monetary order amount and when you served the orders.
- **End of tenancy and deposits:** the move-out date and the forwarding address.

The way you serve a notice decides when the tenant is treated as receiving it: the same day if handed over, 3 days after if attached to the door, left in the mail slot or emailed, and 5 days after if sent by registered mail. **Key dates** works out when the tenant is treated as receiving the notice, their last day to pay or dispute, the effective date, and the earliest day to apply. Dates marked **confirm** should be checked against the RTB's rules before you rely on them.

# Residential: RTB form data sheets

**RTB forms: what goes on each one** lists every detail for the RTB-30, RTB-34, RTB-46, RTB-12L-DR and RTB-44, filled in from your file. Anything still missing shows in red with the section to fill in. Click **Download all data sheets (PDF)**, or **Data sheet PDF** under one form, and copy the details onto the RTB's own forms or into the online application. The RTB only accepts its own forms, which you can get from the links under **Official sources**.

# Commercial leases

A commercial file shows:

- **Where the arrears claim goes:** under {{CRT_MAX}} the Civil Resolution Tribunal, up to {{SMALL_CLAIMS_MAX}} Small Claims (Provincial Court), and above that the file is referred to a lawyer.
- **Lease remedies:** sue for the arrears and keep the lease going, have a bailiff seize the tenant's goods (distress), or end the lease by re-entry if the lease allows it. Pick one and save the date you acted on it. **Key dates** shows when the lease's re-entry right may arise and the limitation date for the oldest unpaid rent.
- **Lease details:** the names, premises, rent, the re-entry period, the deposit and the tenant's contact details.

The commercial checklist takes you through checking the lease, sending the demand, choosing a remedy, applying the deposit and starting the claim. Request lawyer review before distress or a lock-out.

# Lawyer review and closing a file

Click **Request lawyer review (add-on)** on any file to send it to the firm. When the arrears are paid or the matter is over, click **Mark settled**. Open files' dates also show on your **Calendar** as "Rent arrears".
