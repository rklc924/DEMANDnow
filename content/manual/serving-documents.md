After you file a Small Claims Notice of Claim, the defendant has to receive it before the case can move forward. This is called **service**. The **Serving the Notice of Claim** card on the matter page gives you two options.

# Get the name and address right first

Serve the party named on the Notice of Claim, by its exact legal name. For a company, that means its registered office from a current BC Registry company search. For a person, where they live now. See [Confirm who you're pursuing](/help/demand-letters).

# Option 1: Book a process server

A process server delivers the documents for a fixed fee. The current prices for each area are on the matter page. They include up to three attempts, GPS-stamped photos, and a completed Certificate of Service returned to you. A {{SERVICE_BOOKING_FEE}} Demand Now booking fee is added to each job. Service costs can usually be claimed back from the defendant as part of your expenses.

1. Click **Book a process server**.
2. Choose where the defendant is. Tick **Rush** if you need an attempt within 24 hours.
3. Check the name to serve and the last known address. Add notes for the server, such as the best times, a description or the buzzer number.
4. Add the court file number and registry if you have them.
5. Upload your filed Notice of Claim, unless you already uploaded it in the checklist.
6. Click **Book and charge my card**.

**Lower Mainland** jobs go straight to the process server by email and text, with your documents and a pre-filled Certificate of Service. For **other areas** we email you a quote first, and nothing is charged until you accept. The booking's status (with the process server, served, or quote requested) shows on the matter.

# Option 2: Serve it yourself

You, an employee or any adult can deliver the documents.

- **What to serve:** a copy of the filed Notice of Claim, your filed Address for Service (Form 38), a blank Reply (Form 2) and a blank Address for Service (Form 38).
- **Serving an individual:** leave a copy with the person, or send it by registered mail. Registered mail only counts once it's delivered, and you need Canada Post's signature or delivery confirmation as proof.
- **Serving a company:** send it by registered mail to, or leave it at, the company's registered office; leave it at a place of business with a receptionist or the person who appears to be in charge; or leave it with a director or officer.
- **Write it down on the spot:** the date, the address, who you handed it to and how you knew it was them. Take a photo of the location if you can.
- **Serve within 12 months** of filing the Notice of Claim.

# Proof of service: the Certificate of Service

As soon as the documents are served, complete the Certificate of Service (Form 4), have the person who served sign it, and file it at the registry. Download it from the matter page, pre-filled for a **company** or an **individual**. If you served by registered mail, attach Canada Post's signature or delivery confirmation.

Then upload the signed certificate in the checklist step **Serve the Notice of Claim** so Demand Now can check it.

# Once served: your next dates

Enter the date the Notice of Claim was served and click **Work out my dates**. Demand Now shows:

- when the defendant's Reply is due (14 days after service, or 30 days if served outside BC), and
- the earliest date you can apply for a default order if no Reply arrives.

The card also links to the BC Government's Small Claims how-to guides and the Small Claims Rules.
