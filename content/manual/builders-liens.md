The Builders Lien add-on is for contractors, subtrades, workers and suppliers in BC who improved a property and haven't been paid. Under the *Builders Lien Act* you can register a lien against the property you worked on, but only within a short window. Demand Now works out your deadlines, prepares the Claim of Lien details, and can file it for you.

> **Warning:** A claim of lien must be filed within 45 days after the job is completed, abandoned or terminated, or after a certificate of completion is filed. Miss it and the lien is gone. Strata properties have special rules: have a lawyer confirm the deadline.

# Turn it on

Open **Liens** in the menu and click **Turn on Builders Liens**. The deadline tools are included in your plan. You only pay when we file a lien or pull a title search for you.

# Start a lien

Under **Start a lien**, fill in:

- **You are a:** general contractor, subcontractor or subtrade, worker, or material supplier.
- **Your legal name** (the claimant), **who hired you**, **what you supplied** and the **amount owing**.
- The **property**: its address, PID and legal description from the title search, and the **registered owner exactly as on title**. Tick the box if it's a strata property.
- The **critical dates**: the last day you worked or supplied materials (required), and, if you know them, the date a certificate of completion was filed and the date the head contract was completed, abandoned or terminated.

Click **Work out my deadlines**. Your liens are listed with the most urgent deadline at the top.

# Your deadlines

Each lien page shows its deadlines with a countdown:

| Deadline | How it's worked out |
| --- | --- |
| Last day to file the claim of lien | 45 days after the certificate of completion or the end of the head contract. If you only gave your last day of work, it's marked **estimate**: the real deadline can be earlier, so file sooner if you can |
| Start a court action and register a certificate of pending litigation | 1 year after the lien was filed, or the lien expires |
| Respond to a 21-day notice to commence an action | 21 days after you were served with one. If you miss it, the owner can clear the lien from title |
| Last day for a trust claim | 1 year after the head contract ended (estimate) |

Weekend deadlines roll to the next business day. Statutory holidays aren't counted yet, so confirm any deadline that falls near one. Update the dates under **Critical dates** as things happen, especially the date you filed and the date of any notice to commence. Red banners warn you when fewer than 10 days are left, or if a deadline appears to have passed.

# Claim of Lien (Form 5)

The **Claim of Lien (Form 5)** card shows what goes on the form: claimant, owner, the land (PID and legal description), the work or materials, the last day supplied and the amount claimed.

> **Important:** Claim only what is owed for work and materials actually supplied. An inflated lien can make you liable for the owner's costs.

# Where does your lien rank?

Filing a lien doesn't put you first in line. Mortgages and other charges registered before you usually get paid first if the property is sold. A **post-registration title search** confirms your lien is on the right title and shows everything ahead of it, so you know how much security you really have before spending money on a court action. We can pull it for **{{LIEN_TITLE_SEARCH_FEE}}** plus Land Title Office costs. It's offered when you order filing, and again once the lien is filed.

# The lien checklist

1. Confirm your critical dates.
2. Get a current title search for the property (upload it to be checked).
3. Prepare the Claim of Lien (Form 5) and have it signed (upload it to be checked).
4. File it at the Land Title Office before the deadline (upload the registration confirmation).
5. Pull a post-registration title search to see where your lien ranks.
6. Send the owner a copy of the filed lien with a demand for payment.
7. Watch for a 21-day notice to commence an action.
8. Start the court action and register a certificate of pending litigation. This is Supreme Court work, so request lawyer review well before the deadline.

Uploads are checked the same way as on a matter. See [Uploading documents and automatic checks](/help/enforcement). When you're paid, click **Mark paid (lien satisfied)**.
